## Claim-by-claim examination

1. **Medicare IM request precondition — supported wording conflict, not yet approved.** PDF printed p. 8/HTML §2 glossary says: “Medicare patients must request a written discharge notice (‘The Important Message from Medicare’) if they disagree with discharge. If requested, the notice must be provided. Once the notice is provided and if the Medicare patient disagrees with the notice, an appeal can be processed.” CMS's current IM/DND page says hospitals **must deliver the IM to all Medicare inpatients**, including Medicare Advantage, and a **Detailed Notice of Discharge (DND)** is given *only if* the beneficiary asks for an appeal. Current 42 CFR §§405.1205(b)(1), 422.620(b)(1) require an automatic standardized notice at/near admission, no later than 2 calendar days; §405.1206(e)(1) and §422.622(e)(1) require the detailed notice only after QIO appeal. Current NYCRR §405.9(b)(14)(i) says a Medicare inpatient “must receive” the IM upon admission. The glossary conflates these two notices and implies an improper request prerequisite. Counterevidence inside the same PDF, printed p. 12: “Medicare patients receive a copy of the ‘Important Message From Medicare’”; printed p. 4 advises patients to ask for the IM **if they have not received it**, which is sound advice, but then says “You must have this written discharge notice in order to appeal.” Under §405.1206(a)–(b) the patient requests QIO review directly, without a notice-possession precondition; §405.1206(d)(2) makes the QIO check whether notice was validly delivered. Potential consequence is a patient waiting for a missing notice instead of contacting the QIO; no documented denial of one. The actual notice on printed p. 17 gives direct QIO instructions. Review all statements together.
2. **Managed-care plan as first appeal step — supported for MA subgroup, reviewer decision pending.** PDF printed p. 4/HTML §1: “If you are a patient enrolled in an HMO or managed care plan, first request/submit an expedited appeal to the HMO or plan's utilization review committee if … you are being asked to leave the hospital too soon.” This wording does not exclude Medicare Advantage, and the booklet's glossary describes managed care broadly; the immediately preceding paragraph expressly mentions Medicare HMOs. 42 CFR §422.622(a),(b)(1) gives MA enrollees the **direct QIO** route by discharge day; §422.622(b)(5) makes plan expedited reconsideration a route when timely QIO review was not requested, without the same payment protection. CMS's current Part C/D appeal guidance §100.1 and [BFCC-QIO review page](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/appeals-grievances/managed-care/bfcc-qio-review) confirm timely requests bypass plan reconsideration. The embedded IM (PDF pp. 17–18) correctly instructs QIO first, a mitigating internal conflict. A patient following the p. 4 summary could miss the direct QIO deadline. The booklet may have intended this paragraph for non-Medicare managed care, but it does not say so. The reviewer should decide whether the subgroup ambiguity clears the publication threshold.
3. **Non-Medicare appeal deadline — supported conflict, conditions matter.** PDF printed p. 14/HTML §2 non-Medicare admission message: “To avoid extra charges you must call to appeal no later than your planned discharge date.” Official 10 NYCRR §405.9(b)(14)(ii) **mandated notice text** says “by 12 noon of the day after you receive the notice.” Operative §405.9(i)(4)(ii) gives an expedited inpatient review when request is made “not later than noon of the first working day after” receiving written notice. The booklet's date and rule's receipt-based deadline diverge in **both directions**. Example A: notice Monday, planned discharge Wednesday: rule's first-working-day deadline is Tuesday noon, while booklet suggests Wednesday. Example B: notice Friday, planned discharge Saturday: rule says Monday noon if still an inpatient, while booklet suggests Saturday. Do not infer a blanket right to remain inpatient until Monday; whether the patient has left affects the review route. Also §405.9(i)(6) shields **case-based-payment** patients from extra charges during timely review; the booklet's universal “avoid extra charges” phrasing is overbroad for a per-diem private insurer. Obtain a specific payer/context example and independent reviewer verdict before publication.
4. **Universal 24-hour non-Medicare notice — uncertain.** PDF printed p. 8/HTML §2: “A discharge notice must be provided to all patients (except Medicare patients who receive a copy of an ‘Important Message from Medicare’) in writing 24 hours before they leave the hospital.” Current §405.9(i)(3)(i) requires written notice **at the time** hospital and attending physician agree inpatient care is no longer medically necessary and a plan exists. Its operative text contains no blanket 24-hour minimum. This may be an overly protective/administrative practice rather than a rights-denying instruction; no flag without tracing another governing provision or state policy that supplies 24 hours.
5. **Written discharge plan for all — aligned.** PDF pp. 8, 12, 14, 16 and HTML §2 say all patients, including Medicare, receive a written plan before leaving; §405.9(h)(1) and (i)(1) support it, as does §405.9(b)(14)(i). 
6. **Required continuing services secured or reasonably available — aligned in tested scope.** PDF pp. 8, 14, 16; §405.9(h)(1) and (b)(14)(i) match. Does not guarantee a patient's preferred provider or insurance coverage.
7. **Opportunity to participate in planning — aligned in tested scope.** PDF pp. 4, 14, 16 and §405.9(h)(3)(vi),(viii), with exceptions in §405.9(h)(6) when the competent patient objects to family involvement or family cannot be reached/declines.
8. **Non-Medicare written discharge notice and appeal route — aligned broadly.** PDF pp. 12, 14; §405.9(i)(1),(3),(4). The precise deadline is separately disputed in row 3.
9. **Medicare direct QIO route, DND after appeal — aligned in embedded form.** PDF pp. 17–18 says call QIO by planned discharge date and says a detailed notice follows the appeal. CMS's current IM/DND page matches this general sequence; only the glossary misnames the notice. The booklet's embedded form is marked **CMS-R-193 (approved 05/07)** while current CMS requires **CMS-10065** and posts the current version. This is an obsolete example/form issue, potentially important to hospitals that reproduce it, but not proof a hospital actually uses that form. Do not count as a separate patient-rights finding without current-form comparison.
10. **QIO identity/phone — contact remains valid, branding stale.** PDF p. 8 calls Livanta the QIO, and p. 17 gives **1-866-815-5440**. CMS now calls the contractor **Commence Health (formerly Livanta)**; its [official regional contact page](https://www.commencehealthqio.cms.gov/en/Contact-Information) lists that exact number for Region 2, including NY. No actionable wrong-number finding. The same DOH glossary inconsistently says QIO is IPRO; that is an internal naming error, but the actual Medicare appeal phone printed in the form still reaches the correct contractor.
11. **DRG does not automatically impose length-of-stay cap — broadly aligned, wording overgeneral.** PDF p. 8 says DRG “does not limit the number of days” and stay depends “solely on your medical condition.” Official §405.9(b)(14)(ii) itself says discharge is determined by care needs rather than DRG category/insurance; §405.9(h)(7) prohibits discharge based on source of payment. “Solely” elides discharge planning, safe transfer, and review process, but there is no distinct demonstrated false entitlement in this short explanatory sentence. No flag.

## Source-quality notes and publication gate

The PDF p. 15 cites **10 NYCRR §405.9(g)(1),(g)(3)(i)** for discharge notice. In the current 2019-effective official compilation, (g) is **Human Trafficking**; discharge is (h) and inpatient discharge review is (i). This is an outdated citation, not a separately demonstrated change in discharge rights. The live HTML section 1 is older (January 2018 footer) than the PDF (March 2023), yet the disputed text persists in the latter. No current hospital practice, denial of appeal, extra bill, or patient harm was established. The shared official PDF binary, independently reviewer-rendered page images, state rule, and federal text have now been captured. The independent reviewer's verdict is recorded below; the old embedded CMS form remains a held issue.
