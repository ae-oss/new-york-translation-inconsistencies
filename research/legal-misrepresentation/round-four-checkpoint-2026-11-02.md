# New York legal-guidance expansion: 51 enacted sections

**Research date:** 2 November 2026. **Publication status:** Local research only. No new finding is approved or posted to the public website.

## What “51” means

The [inventory](round-four-inventory.json) contains **51 distinct, current New York enacted statutory sections**, not 51 separate Acts, 51 complete statutes, or 51 agency publications. Each section has a direct saved New York Senate page with its displayed latest-revision marker and SHA-256 hash. The 51 came from seven law codes: Real Property 25, Multiple Dwelling 11, Labor 6, General Obligations 4, Real Property Actions and Proceedings 3, Public Service 1, and Penal 1. Previously examined RPP §§ 216 and 226-c, WKC § 28, LAB §§ 196-b and 620, and WKC §§ 203–204 were not counted as additions.

This is a deliberately narrow **guide-passage-to-section triage**. Forty-five entries map to the Attorney General's [Residential Tenants’ Rights Guide](https://ag.ny.gov/publications/residential-tenants-rights-guide), and six to the Labor Department's [Wages and Hours FAQ](https://dol.ny.gov/node/6461). Both English agency pages were directly captured on 2 November; their HTML displays no edition date. The inventory records the guide URL, saved file, hash, short passage or researcher paraphrase, statutory URL, saved file, revision marker, and first-pass disposition. The 44 `targeted_no_apparent_conflict` records mean only that the selected passage did not reveal an obvious contradiction in this first pass. They do not certify full-document or exhaustive provision review. Six records are `source_limited`, and one is a `provisional_conflict`. No language-pair or whole-guide coverage is claimed from this inventory.

The [statute capture manifest](evidence/round-four/manifest.json) also records nine Labor-page fetches blocked by HTTP 429 and one Real Property § 768 URL that returned a generic page without a section text. Those ten attempted pages are excluded from the 51. The [guidance capture manifest](evidence/round-four/guidance-manifest.json) records the Labor FAQ and three other active Labor pages used for a separate overtime issue. The saved Attorney General English and Spanish pages and their hashes are in the [prior capture manifest](evidence/round-three/manifest.json). The apparent printable English PDF link returned the same HTML page, so no current printable PDF edition was established from that link.

## Provisional candidate inside the 51-section triage

The Attorney General's domestic-violence lease-termination passage says a terminating tenant **must** leave the apartment free of any occupants, except for other people on the lease. Current [Real Property Law § 227-c(4)(a)(i)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/RPP/227-C) expressly says a sole leaseholder is **not responsible for ensuring that the abusive household member is absent**. The same-facts case is a sole leaseholder who properly gives notice and leaves but cannot make an abusive household member leave. The guide could wrongly make termination appear unavailable or imply an unsafe removal duty. This is a narrow, high-confidence textual conflict with **potentially major individual consequences**, not evidence of actual reliance or harm. The section page displays a latest revision of 27 December 2019; the current guide HTML was captured 2 November 2026.

The saved [Spanish Attorney General guide](https://es.ag.ny.gov/publications/residential-tenants-rights-guide) repeats the categorical free-of-occupants sentence without the abusive-household-member exception. That is a shared guidance-versus-statute issue in two language versions, **not an English-to-Spanish mistranslation**. Only this corresponding Spanish paragraph was checked. Before any publication, confirm the current official guide rendering and any alternate edition, recheck later amendments, and perform a final legal/context challenge. No claim of actual harm is supported.

## Separate farmworker overtime issue

Three current Labor Department pages—the [Wages and Hours FAQ](https://dol.ny.gov/node/6461), [What is Wage Theft?](https://dol.ny.gov/node/52586), and [File a Complaint](https://dol.ny.gov/node/52591)—say farmworker overtime starts after **56 hours**. The FAQ's own chart correctly states **52 hours from 1 January 2026**. The Department's published adopted [12 NYCRR § 190-2.4(c)](https://dol.ny.gov/farm-laborers-wage-board) also sets 52 hours from that date, corroborated by its 2026 materials. A worker with 54 hours in a covered 2026 workweek could be deterred from seeking the two premium hours by the 56-hour prose. This is **one provisional issue appearing on three pages**, not three findings. It is a guidance-versus-regulation comparison, so it is **outside the 51 enacted statutory sections**. The FAQ chart and linked updated materials partly correct the stale prose; that context must remain visible in any eventual case. No actual underpayment or reliance was documented.

## Independent review and limitations

One independent reviewer monitored the inventory, checked all 51 section-file hashes and distinct citations, challenged both serious candidates, and inspected every fifth inventory record (IDs 05, 10, 15, 20, 25, 30, 35, 40, 45, 50). Seven sampled claims aligned with the relevant statutory clause, one was upgraded to the domestic-violence candidate, and two were kept source-limited. The reviewer specifically verified that RPP § 231(4) supports the furniture-security clause even though the section title and opening paragraphs suggest another subject. This ten-record sample is a quality check, **not an error-detection-rate estimate**.

The inventory is concentrated in two English guidance pages and does not establish a broad New York agency or language coverage rate. Some guide sentences cite several authorities; a single statutory section cannot resolve those combined claims. In particular, the guide's treatment of post-eviction belongings and its summary of child-discrimination exceptions need other-law/context review before a verdict. The four preliminary issues in this and the [preceding checkpoint](round-three-checkpoint-2026-11-02.md) remain separate from the site's **two approved findings**. No new results were deployed.
