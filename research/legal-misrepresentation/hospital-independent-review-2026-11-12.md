# New York hospital patient-rights guidance: independent review

Review date: 2026-11-12. Reviewer role: independently challenge the records and discharge investigators' candidate findings and their coverage claims. This is a bounded examination of the New York Department of Health (DOH) *Your Rights as a Hospital Patient in New York State* booklet, not an audit of all hospital law or all 45 printed pages of the booklet.

## Independent candidate verdicts

| Candidate | Same-facts and exception check | Review verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Records inspection: printed p.7 `within 10 business days` | PHL §18(2)(a) requires *opportunity to inspect* within ten days of written request. GCL §20 ordinarily makes these calendar days; §25-a extends the endpoint if it falls on a Saturday, Sunday, or public holiday. DOH's separate [records rights page](https://www.health.ny.gov/professionals/patients/patient_rights/docs/you_and_your_health_records) and [Publication 1443](https://www.health.ny.gov/publications/1443/index.htm) say 10 days. §18(2)(d) gives copies within a *reasonable time*, not the ten-day inspection period. Official DOH PDF physical p.9 / printed p.7 was visually checked. | **Support as a bounded inspection-deadline finding.** The business-day modifier can substantially lengthen the stated inspection deadline. Do not say the booklet governs copy delivery. |
| Records copying fee: printed p.7 `may charge ... up to 75 cents per page` | PHL §18(2)(e) has a paper-copy cap of 75 cents/page, actual-cost constraint, access despite inability to pay, and no-charge government-benefit request exception. The booklet says `may`, not `must` or `always`. | **Do not publish as a standalone contradiction.** It is incomplete guidance, but the quoted statement can be true in a qualifying case. |
| General DOH records page says providers may charge for `inspections, shipping and copying` | [DOH's general page](https://www.health.ny.gov/professionals/patients/patient_rights/docs/you_and_your_health_records) covers practitioners and facilities and says providers may charge inspection fees. PHL §18(2)(e) generally authorizes reasonable inspection charges. But the hospital-specific current [10 NYCRR §405.7(c)(15)](https://regs.health.ny.gov/content/section-4057-patients-rights) mandates a hospital Patients' Bill of Rights saying a patient may review a medical record without charge, and §405.7(b)(24) authorizes hospital charges for *copies* only. | **Uncertain; hold.** The general statement can be true for practitioners and some facilities but omits the hospital-specific free-review right. Do not frame it as a categorical false permission for every provider. |
| Medicare IM: printed p.8 glossary says patient `must request` IM if disagreeing with discharge; p.4 says patient `must have` it to appeal | 42 CFR §405.1205(b) requires hospital delivery of notice to all Medicare inpatients by day 2 after admission; §405.1205(c) ordinarily requires a follow-up copy before discharge. 42 CFR §405.1206(e) requires a different detailed notice after QIO appeal is requested. §405.1206(a)–(b) gives QIO appeal right without a notice-possession prerequisite, and §405.1206(d)(2) has QIO check whether notice was validly delivered. The same DOH booklet p.4 advises asking for the IM *if not received*, and section 2 says Medicare patients receive an IM. | **Support as a narrowly framed glossary/summary conflict.** The glossary falsely frames IM as request-triggered; p.4 may also discourage a patient from directly appealing if the hospital omitted it. Internal correction and potential IM/DND conflation must be explained. No actual denial established. |
| MA discharge review: printed p.4 says managed-care patient should *first* file expedited HMO/plan appeal | The booklet's HMO references expressly include Medicare patients; the p.6 line says `Medicare managed care enrollees may call Livanta`. Under 42 CFR §422.622(a)–(b), an MA inpatient can request immediate QIO review by discharge day directly, with financial protections tied to timely request. [CMS managed-care QIO guidance](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/appeals-grievances/managed-care/bfcc-qio-review) expressly says timely QIO review bypasses plan reconsideration; plan expedited appeal is an option after a missed QIO deadline. | **Support if confined to MA inpatients contesting discharge.** The p.6 QIO sentence and embedded IM partially correct p.4, so publish the contradiction and full context together; do not imply the rule applies to Medicaid/commercial plans. |
| Non-Medicare appeal timing: printed p.14 says call no later than planned discharge date | The current official §405.9(b)(14)(ii) model notice says noon the day after receipt; §405.9(i)(4)(ii) operative accelerated-review deadline is noon of first working day after receipt. The clocks may diverge in either direction. §405.9(i)(6) expressly limits its payment shield to case-based payment. | **Support; strongest discharge candidate.** Specify non-Medicare inpatient and distinguish model-notice wording from operative working-day rule. Do not claim one universal earlier/later deadline or universal payment loss. |
| Glossary p.8 says non-Medicare notice always given `24 hours before` departure | Need reconcile exact §405.9(i)(3) notice trigger and any insurance-specific/federal exception, and ensure statement is not a simplified minimum advance rule from another authority. | **Hold.** Insufficient verified basis to call it wrong. |

## Coverage and limitations

The bounded audit focuses on the booklet's records-access passage (printed p.7), discharge guidance (printed pp.4, 8, and 14–17), and relevant glossary text. Other portions include general patient rights, pediatric, maternity, advance directives, domestic violence, billing, consent, and medical misconduct. They have **not** been systematically checked, so a site page must not say that all material claims in the booklet or healthcare sector were reviewed.

The reviewer sampled unflagged claims in the same bounded passages: (1) the 75-cent paper-copy cap, which is accurate only as a ceiling and subject to exceptions; (2) the statement that a patient may appeal denial of record access, consistent with §18; (3) the Medicare IM at-admission requirement, supported by §405.1205; and (4) the written discharge-plan right, corroborated by the booklet's separate statutory notice and §405.9. This is a purposive challenge sample, not a statistical estimate of missed-error rate.

The booklet's `Livanta` label appears stale because [CMS now calls the contractor Commence Health (formerly Livanta)](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/quality/quality-improvement-organizations/family-centered-care). Its [current Region 2 site](https://www.commencehealthqio.cms.gov/) still lists the same 866-815-5440 number printed in the booklet for New York. This is not a standalone material finding on present evidence.

No documented downstream injury or agency reliance has been found in this independent review. Consequences described above are plausible potential effects of following the guidance, not observed outcomes. The three discharge conflicts and one records-inspection deadline conflict are supported as *bounded candidate findings* after text/image/source comparison. Publication requires the parent editor to preserve the qualifications and cite the exact legal provisions; the fee and universal 24-hour leads remain on hold.

## Final editorial decision on the four supported cases

I read the completed [records investigator packet](hospital-records-investigation-2026-11-12.md) (14 targeted checks across three distinct guidance documents) and [discharge investigator packet](hospital-discharge-investigation-2026-11-12.md) (11 targeted claims in the DOH booklet) after conducting my own source reopening. I approve the following **for consideration as four separately identified site findings**, with these bounds:

| Finding | Recommended potential significance | Confidence in guidance–rule conflict | Required qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| DOH booklet says 10 **business** days to inspect hospital records | **Limited to moderate**. An extra few days can matter in a time-sensitive case, but no such case was documented. | **High**: PDF image, statute, day-count provisions, hospital regulation, and DOH's own other guidance agree. | Inspection *opportunity* only; exclude request day, and apply weekend/holiday endpoint rule. Do not say copies must arrive in ten days. |
| Non-Medicare appeal instruction uses planned discharge date | **Major potential** when the erroneous date causes a missed expedited-review deadline or a mistaken early appeal; no actual outcome documented. | **High** on conflict; **moderate** on individual payment consequences because reimbursement and timing facts vary. | §405.9(i)(4)(ii) operative deadline is noon of first working day after notice receipt; mandated form says noon day after receipt. The guidance can be too late *or* unnecessarily early. §405.9(i)(6) payment shield is case-based. |
| Managed-care paragraph steers Medicare Advantage inpatient to plan first | **Major potential** because timely direct QIO review has a deadline and payment protection; no actual outcome documented. | **Moderate–high**: direct federal rule is clear, but the booklet's generic `managed care` paragraph may have been intended for a narrower group. | Apply only to Medicare Advantage **inpatients** contesting discharge. Publish the same booklet's p.6 QIO phone and embedded IM instructions as counterevidence. The correct current QIO brand is Commence Health (formerly Livanta), with the booklet's number still valid. |
| Medicare IM glossary frames automatic notice as request-triggered, and p.4 suggests it is needed before appeal | **Limited to moderate**: delay or confusion is plausible, but the booklet elsewhere states the IM is given and provides the QIO route. | **High** on the glossary's conflict with automatic notice duty; **moderate** on practical effect due internal corrections. | Distinguish the automatic Important Message (IM) from the appeal-triggered Detailed Notice of Discharge (DND). State that patients may contact the QIO even if the hospital failed to supply an IM. |

Each row is a discrepancy between government guidance and a governing rule, **not a finding that a hospital violated the rule**. None rests on translation. The four site entries should cite the March 2023 PDF, exact pages, current governing provisions, same-booklet corrective passages, and a clear `potential consequence` label. No verified downstream incidents were found.

### Held and rejected leads

- **Hold:** general DOH records page's omission of free hospital record review under §405.7(c)(15). Its inspection-fee sentence is true for some nonhospital providers under §18; hospital applicability is not sufficiently explicit for a categorical finding.
- **Hold:** booklet's universal 24-hour non-Medicare notice claim. §405.9(i)(3) speaks to notice when discharge determinations are made, but an additional policy or rule could supply more advance notice; no rights-denying discrepancy was proved.
- **Hold:** old CMS-R-193 form image and old §405.9(g) cross-reference in the booklet. These demonstrate stale examples/citations but no actual hospital use of an obsolete form or changed patient right was proved.
- **Reject as separate findings:** booklet's 75-cent-per-page `may charge` summary, the old `Livanta` brand with a still-working Region 2 number, the DRG/length-of-stay simplification, and all aligned claims in the investigator ledgers.

### Review-coverage accounting

The 14 records checks and 11 discharge checks are **targeted claim checks**, not 25 pages or 25 unique source documents. The reviewers examined the contested passages and a purposive sample of unflagged claims, including copy request timing, records denial appeals, current complaint number, discharge-plan availability, opportunity to participate, and current QIO contact. The negative sample cannot establish a site-wide miss rate. The rest of DOH publication 1449, other DOH guidance, NY statutes and regulations, CMS guidance, other insurers, and actual hospital implementation remain unscreened.
