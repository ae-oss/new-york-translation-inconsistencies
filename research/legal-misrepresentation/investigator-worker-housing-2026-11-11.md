## Candidate WCB-6A — hearing/absence instruction

- **English C-3 p4, claimant steps item 5:** “Go to all hearings when notified to appear.”
- **Spanish C-3 p4, item 5:** “Preséntese a todas las ausencias cuando reciba notificación de comparecencia.”
- **Literal Spanish meaning:** “Appear at all absences when you receive a notice to appear.” The expected hearing term is *audiencias*. The same Spanish C-3 uses *audiencia(s)* correctly in question A7 (p1) and its note (p3), showing this is a localized mistake rather than a distinct name for hearings.
- **Governing context:** [WCL §20](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/WKC/20) provides for Board hearings in claim determination. The C-3 English instruction and [WCB hearing guide](https://www.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/hearings.jsp) direct participation; this record does not establish a general personal-attendance legal duty or quantify consequences of missing a hearing.
- **Assessment sent for review:** Clear lexical mistranslation; **limited potential clarity impact**. The rest of the sentence mentions a notice to appear, so a reader may still understand it. No evidence of an actual missed hearing or altered legal outcome. Prior C-3 work covered a representative-fee clause; this is a separate, previously unrecorded clause, **not** a newly discovered document.

## Candidate HCR-6B — fire-escape-window emergency list

- **English FS14 p2:** “window to fire escape (does not open)” in the emergency-condition list.
- **Spanish FS14 numbered p1 / PDF p2:** “ventana de incendios (no se abre).” Literal: “fire window (does not open).” It omits the window's connection to a **fire escape**; the same Spanish list later correctly calls fire escapes *escaleras de incendio*.
- **Policy context:** The [HCR FS14 English](https://hcr.ny.gov/fact-sheet-14) treats listed complaints as first priority. [HCR Advisory Opinion 2020-1](https://hcr.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2020/03/advisory-op-2020-1-final-version.pdf) also names an inoperable window to a fire escape as an emergency condition. Applicable rent-service regulations recognize emergency repairs but this precise list is an agency classification, not established here as statutory text.
- **Assessment sent for review:** Possible limited clarity problem for emergency complaint priority. An alternate reader might infer the fire-escape connection from context; actual impact unverified. Not ready for publication without review.

## Candidate HCR-6C — succession family definition

- **English FS30 p1:** the listed kin are family members “of the tenant or permanent tenant.” The next paragraph includes other persons “residing with the tenant or permanent tenant” who prove emotional and financial commitment and interdependence.
- **Spanish FS30 p1:** the kin list ends “del inquilino, así como un inquilino permanente.” The next paragraph says “cualquier otra persona que viva con el inquilino o sea un inquilino permanente.”
- **Literal Spanish meaning:** The kin are of the tenant “as well as a permanent tenant”; another person may live with the tenant “or be a permanent tenant.” These clauses can read as giving a permanent tenant family status, rather than referring to a family member **of** or co-resident **with** a permanent tenant. Alternative elliptical reading in Spanish needs independent assessment.
- **Governing context:** [HCR's English RA-23.5 form](https://hcr.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2021/06/ra-23.5-fillable.pdf) and [HCR's rent-code amendment text](https://hcr.ny.gov/rsc-rule-text) tie succession to family status and qualifying co-residency under 9 NYCRR §§2520.6(o), 2523.5(b). The online code copy is not a verified official current print supplement, and no legal entitlement is inferred from the translation alone.
- **Assessment sent for review:** Potentially meaningful definitional distortion; practical reading and impact **unassigned pending challenge**. The Spanish introduction still mentions family and residency thresholds, which may resolve ambiguity for a careful reader. A related, separate scope lead is English “rent stabilized and rent controlled” versus Spanish “renta estabilizada y renta regulada” in the introduction; this is under reviewer assessment and is not counted as a supported finding here.

## Candidate HCR-6D — attorney fees versus lawful fees

- **English FS44 p1 summary and p2 detail:** “Legal fees can only be collected from a tenant if ordered by a judge in court.” In context, *legal fees* means attorney/court fees; the same fact sheet lists many other lawful fees.
- **Spanish FS44 p1/p2:** “Solamente pueden cobrarse cuotas legales a un inquilino si así lo ordena un juez.” Literally, “Lawful/legal fees may be charged to a tenant only if a judge so orders.” The document also uses *cuotas legales* as the heading for **lawful fees** generally, so the Spanish can misleadingly extend the court-order condition to every lawful fee.
- **Context and alternative reading:** Other Spanish bullets allow late fees, screening fees, and detector fees in stated conditions, which contradicts the broad reading. A reader may understand *cuotas legales* as attorney fees from the mention of a judge, although *honorarios de abogado* would be clearer. The [Attorney General tenant guide](https://ag.ny.gov/publications/residential-tenants-rights-guide) distinguishes legal fees from other added fees and court proceedings.
- **Reviewer challenge:** The Spanish section itself lists late and background fees that may be charged without a judge, weakening any broad reading of *cuotas legales*. A separate [2025 HCR PAR decision](https://hcr.ny.gov/overcharge-r-pars-january-2-march-31-2025-part-2) describes legal fees collectable on court **or HCR** order, raising a question about the English fact sheet as well. **Status: uncertain/source-conflicted; do not publish** as a clean translation finding without resolving the governing rule and context. No actual improper payment or refusal is documented.

## Coverage and rejections

- **Six unique English documents; six downloaded Spanish translations; six source/edition verified pairs.** This is a targeted clause screen for C-3 and HCR, and a complete three-page high-consequence clause screen for C-32.1. It is not a claim that every word of all twelve PDFs received independent examination.
- **WCB C-3:** injury/illness, medical care, interpreter request, and most numbered claimant-rights clauses did not produce additional supported translation flags. The already known representative-fee wording is deliberately excluded from a new-candidate count.
- **WCB C-32.1:** a separate pass covered the withdrawal window, settlement finality, medical waiver, payment timing, modification, and acknowledgment questions in both current three-page forms. No candidate advanced. This is an unflagged high-consequence sample, not evidence that the translations are globally error-free.
- **HCR FS20:** seven topic clusters listed above appeared aligned in the matched pair; no finding proposed. English “signer” versus Spanish “intérprete de lenguaje de señas” appears to be an English drafting typo clarified by Spanish, not a translation error claimed against a legal rule.
- **HCR FS14:** rent-reduction timing, complaints, and two numerical examples aligned in sampled wording; the fire-escape-window terminology alone remains a candidate.
- **HCR FS30:** one-year/two-year co-residency rules, temporary-absence exceptions, and 12-month owner inquiry limit aligned in sampled wording; family-definition issue remains a candidate.
- **HCR FS44:** numerical late-fee and screening-fee caps, several lawful/unlawful fee examples, and the complaint route aligned in sampled wording; attorney-fee terminology remains a candidate.
- **Limit:** Only Spanish pairs were compared. The WCB and HCR indexes list other languages, but those links have **not** been edition-matched or screened here. No missed-error rate can be inferred from this small targeted sample.
