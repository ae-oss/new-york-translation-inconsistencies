## Provisional issue 1: DFS flyers shorten an external-appeal deadline

Two different DFS publications, [*Your Right to Appeal a Health Plan Decision*](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/09/Health-Plan-Decision.pdf) and [*Understanding Out-of-Network Benefits*](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/09/OON-Benefits.pdf), both marked “Updated August 2025,” tell an insured patient to file an external appeal within **120 days** after receiving a final adverse determination. These are distinct PDFs with distinct hashes, but **one repeated underlying wording problem**. The current [DFS external-appeal page](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/complaints/file_external_appeal) says **four months**, as do [Insurance Law § 4914(b)(1)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ISC/4914) and [Public Health Law § 4914(2)(a)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBH/4914). [General Construction Law § 30](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GCN/30) computes months as calendar months. For a determination received 1 June 2026, 120 days ends 29 September, while four calendar months ends 1 October. A reader relying only on the flyer might abandon a still-timely appeal in that interval.

**Assessment:** High confidence in the textual discrepancy; **provisional major potential impact** because an appeal opportunity is at stake, although the shortened window may be only a few days and no actual rejection, reliance, or harm has been shown. The flyers link to the correct live DFS appeal page, which may mitigate the error. This is a guidance-versus-law conflict, not an English/target-language mistranslation.

## Provisional issue 2: current DFS translated applications use older editions

The same [DFS external-appeal page](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/complaints/file_external_appeal) links an English hard-copy application marked **EA 04/23** and 13 translated applications. Every linked translated PDF has an **EA 02/18** or **EA 09/18** footer. This is an edition inventory, not a claim that every language has been fully compared. The linked [English application](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/complaints/file_external_appeal/application-english) has a “No Surprises Act (NSA)” denial option in Question 12 and exempts that option from the physician-attestation instruction. The linked [Spanish application](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/05/External_Appeal_Spanish.pdf) has no NSA option and directs an attestation for every listed denial reason except medical necessity. The English and Spanish first pages were checked visually in saved PDF images.

The reviewer and lead researcher checked Question 12's structure in all **13** translated PDFs linked on the current DFS page. PDF text extraction finds seven denial-reason boxes in each translated form, while the English EA04/23 form has eight, including a labeled NSA choice. The absence of an NSA **Question 12 option** was semantically checked in [Spanish](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/05/External_Appeal_Spanish.pdf), [Traditional-script Chinese](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/05/External_Appeal_Chinese.pdf), [French](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/05/External_Appeal_French.pdf), [Russian](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2023/05/External_Appeal_Russian.pdf), and [Hindi](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/03/External_Appeal_Hindi.pdf). The English and Spanish fields were also checked visually in rendered page images. The other eight forms received a structural check only; we did **not** adjudicate which individual reason label differs in those languages. The [form manifest](evidence/round-five/dfs-translated-forms-manifest.json) lists all 13 languages and exact source links. Hindi mentions NSA in Question 13, so the omission claim is specifically about Question 12. The Chinese PDF visibly uses Traditional characters; no broader spoken-language variety was established. The current DFS page separately says NSA-related denials may be externally appealed. A patient using one of the five semantically checked translated forms has no Question 12 box matching that denial. The Spanish form's instructions may also imply that a physician attestation is needed for an NSA appeal, because its exception list lacks NSA. We did not assess the attestation instructions in the other 12 languages. The Spanish and English forms agree on the four-month patient and 60-day provider deadlines. These are narrow checks, **not** full translation audits of every form.

The linked [English application instructions](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/complaints/file_external_appeal/helpful_hints) explain NSA denial reasons for Question 12 and say providers may not file NSA appeals for themselves. The linked [Spanish instructions](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2024/02/External-Appeal-Helpful-Hints_Spanish.pdf) omit both points in the corresponding paragraphs. This reinforces the same English/Spanish application-content gap, not a new separate finding. The PDF URL's `2024/02` path is an upload location; it does not establish an edition date.

**Assessment:** High confidence in the seven-versus-eight Question 12 structure and older edition markers across 13 forms, with the NSA choice's absence semantically checked in five. **Potential impact remains unassigned** pending evidence about the actual submission path and cure process. This is a **current-edition parity problem**, not a mistranslation between matched 2018 and 2023 editions and not 13 separate findings. Older [11 NYCRR § 410.7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/11-NYCRR-410.7) says a timely application is not made incomplete solely by a missing physician attestation and calls for follow-up on missing information. That may mitigate the Spanish attestation wording, but it does not establish how DFS currently processes a paper form with no NSA box. Nor have we established whether the online portal supplies equivalent translated choices or whether newer translated forms exist outside the currently linked set. A bounded search found no newer Spanish application. No actual denial or delay has been documented.

## Provisional issue 3: DOH page states 45 days instead of four months

The DOH [Complaints and Appeals index](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/complaints/) directly links an [External Appeals page](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/appealext.htm) saying applications **must be filed within 45 days** of a managed-care enrollee's final adverse determination or waiver of internal appeal. The page footer says “Revised: November 2024”; this does not prove when the specific paragraph was last edited. The current [Public Health Law § 4914(2)(a)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/PBH/4914) gives the enrollee **four months** on precisely those triggers. Its separate provider rule is 60 days. The DOH [July 2025 model final-adverse-determination notice](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/plans/appeals/2025_july/fad_no_ac.pdf) and DFS's current appeal page both say four months. The model notice is a template, not evidence that a particular plan sent it.

**Assessment:** Independent review confirmed high confidence in the same-stage textual conflict; **provisional major potential impact** because the 45-day wording could deter a still-timely appeal. For a final determination received 1 June 2026, 45 days would point to about 16 July, versus 1 October under four calendar months. This is a guidance-versus-law issue, separate from the DFS 120-day flyers, although both concern external-appeal timing. The primary `www.health.ny.gov` URL returned HTTP 403 to direct automated retrieval; the official `healthweb-back.health.ny.gov` version returned HTTP 200, and the main URL was indexed by search. The live DOH complaints index provides a route to the page, but traffic and actual reliance are unknown. No special 45-day enrollee subset was found in the checked current law or notices. The page references [Chapter 586 of 1998](https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/appeal/chap586.pdf), which used 45 days originally. [Senate bill S5800, signed as Chapter 219 of 2011](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2011/s5800), amended the enrollee period to four months; the [Senate's 2013 bill history](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2013/2013-s5256) expressly describes that extension. An older [11 NYCRR § 410.7 compilation](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/11-NYCRR-410.7) and DOH-hosted [10 NYCRR Subpart 98-2 text](https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/appeal/98-2regs.htm) still print 45-day language. Their present formal status and interaction with the amended statute were not established from the accessible records. This is an unresolved regulatory conflict, not evidence that a current 45-day enrollee exception applies or that a court has invalidated a regulation.

## Checks that did not become findings

- The WCB disability page's short “file within 30 days” instruction was not treated as absolute forfeiture. [Workers' Compensation Law § 217](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/WKC/217) explicitly allows some late claims, while the WCB [statement of rights](https://www.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/forms/db271s.pdf) explains the two-week payment limit and possible excuse. The [DB-450](https://www.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/forms/db450.pdf) cautions about loss of benefits rather than declaring every late claim void. Its instruction to wait for a clinician to finish Part B may warrant usability follow-up but did not support a legal-conflict verdict in this pass.
- The DOH Medicaid managed-care handbook's 120-day *fair-hearing* period was not confused with the *external-appeal* period; the [federal Medicaid rule](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-438/subpart-F/section-438.408) permits a 90–120-day fair-hearing request window. A DOH 2026 chart's seven-day authorization timing still needs the plan rating-period start to establish its exact transition date; it is source-limited, not flagged.
- The [OTDA SNAP Source Book](https://otda.ny.gov/programs/snap/SNAPSB.pdf) distinguishes the 90-day SNAP hearing request from the 60-day agency decision clock; sampled language aligned with [7 CFR § 273.15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-7/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-273/subpart-F/section-273.15). Its notice appendix distinguished Public Assistance and SNAP periods.
- Sampled DHR age, housing, employment, and harassment materials stated the applicable three-year filing rule with the transition where relevant, consistent with [Executive Law § 297(5)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/297). Sampled HCR lease renewal windows, tenant response, and security-deposit terms aligned with the corresponding current rent-stabilization provisions and [General Obligations Law § 7-107](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GOB/7-107). The review was limited to the identified clauses.
- The DHR [FY2023–24 annual report](https://dhr.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/01/nys-dhr-sfy-2024-annual-report.pdf) contradicts itself: its timeline says the general three-year complaint period covers claims from **15 February 2024**, while the following narrative says **15 February 2025**. [Chapter 656 of 2023](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2023/S3255) was signed on 17 November 2023 and took effect on the ninetieth day, 15 February 2024, for claims arising then or later; DHR's current [complaint regulation](https://dhr.ny.gov/465.3-Complaint) agrees. The reviewer classed the annual-report sentence as a **limited historical-report error**, not a major current-guidance finding. A person reading it in isolation could infer a shorter period for a 2024 claim, but the report's adjacent correct timeline mitigates that risk. No reliance or harm is known.
- A separate DOH [model handbook explicitly revised for 2010](https://healthweb-back.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/medicaid_managed_model_member_handbook.htm) contains a 45-day external-appeal statement. Its age and obsolete agency name make it unsuitable as evidence of current instructions; it is excluded from the 36-row ledger and from current-rule findings.
