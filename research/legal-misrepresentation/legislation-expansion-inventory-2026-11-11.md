# New York legislation expansion inventory — 11 November 2026

## What this list means

This is a **candidate research inventory**, not a claim that the listed laws have been fully screened or that any new inconsistency exists. A statutory section is a comparison target; an English agency document and a particular translated edition become a verified pair only after their issuing authority, edition, and text are checked. The project compares agency publications with governing law (Model A) and can later assess government-published translations of enacted law (Model B). A translation of a fact sheet is not itself a translated enactment.

The earlier round-four inventory already examined 51 statutory sections by targeted claim checks. The other recorded work includes selected unemployment, workers' compensation, housing, and health-insurance sections. The packages below emphasize **different sections, different agencies, or different publications**. Reusing a known section to test a new publication is identified as follow-up rather than counted as a new law.

**Source status key:** **Linked** = an agency page visibly links English and translated publications in at least some languages; **Hub** = official publication hub exists but the relevant language pairs/editions remain to be matched; **Conditional** = statutory target is plausible but a suitable translated agency source has not been established. Linked does not mean edition-matched or screened.

## First two-hour sequence

1. **First 25 minutes — select and lock source pairs.** Start with WCB C-3/claimant packet, one HCR rent fact sheet or lease rider, NYSED December 2025 guidance, and the BOE absentee form. Save URLs, issuing agency, document ID, visible edition/footer, PDF hash, language/script, and retrieval date. Reject mismatched editions or non-government translations.
2. **Next 55 minutes — focused legal-meaning screening.** Compare high-consequence clauses: eligibility, deadlines, exceptions, required notices, benefit amount, waiver, and appeal routes. Use the exact governing provision and any implementing regulation. Record original text and independent translation, not just a paraphrase.
3. **Next 25 minutes — independent challenge.** Recheck every candidate against full paragraph context, alternative meanings, current law, and other official versions. Keep an unflagged-passage sample to examine for missed differences.
4. **Final 15 minutes — account for coverage.** Report unique documents, verified English/target pairs, pages or clauses screened, candidate findings by language and status, rejected flags, source gaps, and potential versus demonstrated consequences. Publish no new finding without separate final review and the user's publication approval.

## Recommended starting order

**First wave:** WCB claim forms; HCR lease rider/fact sheets; NYSED immigrant-student and English-learner parent guidance; DOH patient and survivor-rights publications; BOE ballot and confidential-registration applications; DOL minimum-wage fact sheets. These already have visible government-hosted translations and consequences a reader could act on.

**Second wave:** unemployment, disability/Paid Family Leave, wage protection, Good Cause Eviction, security deposits, and health-insurance review. These need more work on edition matching, applicable rules, or an existing provisional issue.

**Verification queue:** public assistance, civil rights, child support, meal/rest, and whistleblower tracks. Do not report a multilingual comparison merely because a translated page might exist.

## Boundaries and counting rules

- New York's [Executive Law §202-a](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/EXC/202-A) concerns translation of vital agency documents. It does **not** by itself establish that enacted legislation is available in all 12 languages.
- For Chinese, record the document's actual script and any stated variety. Do not collapse Simplified and Traditional forms or infer spoken variety from script alone.
- For every pair, record the source's authority and edition; HCR expressly describes its translations as informational. A government-hosted translation can be useful without being legally authoritative.
- Count **unique source documents, translation links, verified pairs, completed clause/page comparisons, and supported findings** separately. Do not count located translations as screened or wording differences as legal consequences.
- No source on this list has been changed on the live website by this inventory.
