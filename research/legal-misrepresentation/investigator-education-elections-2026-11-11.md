## Pending lead E3 — school district counsel described as prosecutor

This was identified after the reviewer completed the first adjudication and is **not cleared for publication**. In the [January 2025 English edition](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/oag-go-sed-immigration-students.pdf), Section III step (3) directs personnel to give documentation to the superintendent and “school district attorney.” The matched [January 2025 Spanish PDF](https://www.nysed.gov/sites/default/files/programs/student-support-services/oag-go-sed-immigration-students-spanish.pdf), page 4, says “superintendente y al fiscal del distrito escolar.” In ordinary legal Spanish, *fiscal* denotes a public prosecutor, not the school district's counsel; the [RAE definition](https://dle.rae.es/fiscal) confirms that usage. The [current Attorney General Spanish HTML page](https://es.ag.ny.gov/education-students/safeguarding-rights-immigrant-students) repeats the same wording, while the current English guidance still refers to the school district attorney. A literal reading could send sensitive records or law-enforcement requests to the wrong office. This is a **guidance recommendation** framed as something staff “should consider,” not a proven statutory duty or documented downstream event. Independent reviewer must check context, possible alternative sense of *fiscal*, exact source alignment, and consequence before assigning any rating or adding it to the site. The January English comparator is local `/private/tmp/oag-go-sed-immigration-students-jan2025.pdf`, SHA-256 `278bb2e16b9148db897888927d3a7df69c7e2510935ae2161532a6ad18f38e0c`.

## Candidate E2 — Spanish election affirmations omit materiality

- **Official absentee pair:** [English 2024 form](https://elections.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/09/absentee-ballot-application-english-fillable.pdf) and [Spanish 2024 form](https://elections.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/09/absentee-ballot-application-spanish-fillable.pdf), each 2 pages and linked from the [BOE ballot page](https://elections.ny.gov/request-ballot). The `/2026/09/` URL path is an upload location; both PDF footers say 2024. In applicant and witness affirmations on page 1, English says “material false statement”; Spanish says “alguna declaración falsa” (literally “any false statement”).
- **Official early-mail pair:** [English 2024 form](https://elections.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/09/early-mail-ballot-english-fillable.pdf) and [Spanish 2024 form](https://elections.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2026/09/early-vote-by-mail-spanish-fillable_0.pdf), 2 pages each; same phrase difference appears in applicant and witness affirmations. This reinforces **one repeated translation pattern**, not another standalone finding.
- **Governing law:** [Election Law §8-400(5),(7)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/8-400) specifies materiality in absentee applicant and witness statements; [§8-700(6),(8)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/8-700) does the same for early mail. [§17-132(6)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/ELN/17-132) uses materiality in the absentee false-statement offense.
- **Meaning and limit:** Spanish expands the wording of the warning from a *material* false statement to *any* false statement. The statute controls actual liability, so this does **not** expand criminal law. A reader might overestimate exposure for an immaterial mistake. However, the English absentee form's separate general instruction also says “a false statement” without materiality, weakening a claim that the misleading impression is unique to Spanish. Potential impact is therefore at most **limited** without evidence of actual deterrence.
- **Source-verification block:** BOE PDFs were available for text extraction in the web tool, but the Spanish PDF screenshot failed with cache miss and direct public `curl` returned HTTP 403; binary hashes and Spanish page-image checks are missing. The in-app browser PDF viewer also remained blank. **Do not publish this candidate as confirmed.**
