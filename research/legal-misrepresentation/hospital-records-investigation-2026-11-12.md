### Supported finding B2: exact operative comparison and consequences

- DOH booklet, printed p. 7: “within 10 business days of your written request.”
- PHL §18(2)(a): “a health care provider shall provide an opportunity, within ten days, for such subject to inspect any patient information” covered by the request.
- GCL §20: a specified period “means such number of calendar days exclusive of the calendar day from which the reckoning is made.” GCL §25-a gives the next business day only when the **last day** is a Saturday, Sunday or public holiday.
- The booklet's extra word *business* can make a patient or hospital calculate a later inspection opportunity than the statute allows. For example, absent a holiday, a written request received on a Monday ordinarily reaches day 10 on the Thursday of the following week; 10 business days ordinarily reaches the Monday after that, four calendar days later. Actual deadline computation depends on receipt and holidays. Delay may matter to care coordination, benefit claims, or rights review; no actual patient harm or hospital practice has been documented. Suggested significance: **limited-to-moderate potential impact**, subject to the project's final rating standard. Confidence in the textual conflict: **high**.

### Uncertain candidate Y2: hospital inspection charges

- General DOH page: “Providers are permitted to charge reasonable fees to recover costs for inspections, shipping and copying.”
- Hospital rule §405.7(c)(15): “Review your medical record without charge”; §405.7(b)(24): hospital can impose reasonable charges for *copies*. This is also reproduced in the booklet itself, physical PDF p. 15.
- The general page is accurate for some practitioners under §18(2)(e). Its omission of the hospital-specific free-inspection rule is potentially misleading to hospital patients, but should **not** be framed as a false rule for all providers. No documented hospital charge or patient consequence was found. Suggested status: **hold/uncertain** pending reviewer decision; not an approved public finding in this investigator packet.

## Broader booklet inventory and coverage boundary

The DOH booklet's section 1 also covers: general participation and explanation rights; language and disability assistance; hospital-care complaints; discharge notice, discharge planning and appeals for non-Medicare, Medicare and managed-care patients; professional-misconduct complaints; hospital bills and insurance; and a glossary. Section 2 reproduces additional required notices and forms, including the Patients' Bill of Rights. I read section 1's table of contents and identified these clusters. **Only its medical-records section and the embedded Bill of Rights item 15 received a substantive law comparison in this packet.** The current DOH complaint phone number was checked; other complaints, discharge, billing, language-access and professional-conduct claims remain outside this packet and must not be reported as fully screened here.

No federal HIPAA comparison was needed to resolve B2: the state statute and New York day-count law establish the more specific inspection deadline. Federal access law was not counted as screened.
