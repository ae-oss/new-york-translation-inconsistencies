### Medicaid managed care

The [DOH model handbook](https://www.health.ny.gov/health_care/managed_care/medicaid/mmc_model_member_handbook.htm) says a member has **120 calendar days** from a Final Adverse Determination to request a State fair hearing. Current [42 CFR § 438.408(f)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-438/subpart-F/section-438.408) requires a window of at least 90 and at most 120 days after the plan's appeal-resolution notice. A displayed [New York regulation page](https://regs.health.ny.gov/content/section-360-108-fair-hearings) still uses a 60-day period for certain managed-care hearing requests. This apparent conflict requires a current-state-rule/adoption and preemption check. It is **not** a finding that the handbook is wrong.

The handbook's ten-day/effective-date language for continued benefits broadly aligns with [42 CFR § 438.420](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-42/chapter-IV/subchapter-C/part-438/subpart-F/section-438.420), which also imposes several conditions that a short handbook summary cannot be read to erase. Plan-specific notices and other Medicaid programs were not screened.

### Rent stabilization

[HCR Fact Sheet 16 (07/2025)](https://hcr.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/07/fact-sheet-16-07-2025.pdf) gives a 35-day period for a Petition for Administrative Review. HCR's [Appealing an Order](https://hcr.ny.gov/node/331) page gives the same period, measured from issuance, and the [online 9 NYCRR § 2529.2 copy](https://govt.westlaw.com/nycrr/Document/I4f8cead1cd1711dda432a117e6e0f345?viewType=FullText) agrees. That online code copy is explicitly unofficial; the current official printed supplement was not inspected. Fact Sheet 16's six-year treble-damages statement was logged as evidence-limited because its pre/post-2019 distinctions, exceptions, and ETPA reach need a fuller statute-and-code check. The [2025 Fact Sheet 26](https://hcr.ny.gov/system/files/documents/2025/07/fact-sheet-26-07-2025.pdf) was located but could not be stably downloaded in this pass.

### Paid Family Leave

The program's [eligibility page](https://paidfamilyleave.ny.gov/eligibility) gives the usual 26-consecutive-week threshold for employees working at least 20 hours a week, alongside the 175-day threshold for regular schedules below 20 hours. Those selected statements align with current [Workers' Compensation Law § 203](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/WKC/203). A separate construction-worker provision takes effect in 2027 and was not treated as current law. The [2026 program page](https://paidfamilyleave.ny.gov/2026) states a 12-week/67% benefit with the state-wage cap, consistent with [§ 204(2)(a)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/WKC/204) and the [Board's 2026 announcement](https://apps.wcb.ny.gov/content/main/PressRe/paid-family-leave-2026.jsp). No full claims-process or insurer-decision audit was performed.
