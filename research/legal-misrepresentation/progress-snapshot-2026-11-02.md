## Recommended repeatable progress markers

For each subsequent batch, report: (1) unique source publications located, (2) exact editions and language pairs verified, (3) selected legally consequential claims compared with a currently effective statute or regulation, (4) sampled unflagged claims independently challenged, (5) issue clusters supported, uncertain, rejected, or source-limited, and (6) findings approved for publication. Count full-document screening only if the whole document was actually examined. Treat repeated wording across several files as one underlying issue, with all affected publications listed.

**Research direction:** Start with current agency guidance and forms linked to enacted rights and deadlines, then work backwards to the governing law. This yields concrete, comparable statements and a clear legal consequence test. Use enacted sections as authority for those tests rather than counting statutes without a relevant public-facing publication. Prioritize language comparisons only when the editions, document identity, and translation provenance match.
